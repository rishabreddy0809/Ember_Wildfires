In one day, NASA satellites flagged 136,676 hot spots on Earth. Which ones are real wildfires?

Ember combines every MODIS and VIIRS detection from NASA FIRMS and uses machine learning to sort real wildfires from false alarms.

01 · The signal

Every point on this globe is a real detection

These are all 136,676 detections NASA’s FIRMS system recorded from MODIS and NOAA-20 VIIRS on Nov 7–8, 2026. Most are in Africa, where seasonal burning is common.

Satellites don’t see flames. They spot thermal anomalies: pixels much hotter than the ground around them. Bigger points released more fire radiative power.

02 · Two sensors

MODIS and VIIRS see the ground differently

MODIS rides on Terra and Aqua with pixels about 1 km wide. VIIRS rides on Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 with pixels about 375 m wide (Giglio et al., 2016; Schroeder et al., 2014).

Each one catches fires the other misses, so Ember uses both. (Orbits drawn here are illustrations.)

03 · The mess

One fire becomes many detections

Zoom into California on the same day: 133 detections. Put both sensors on one map and a single fire turns into a pile of overlapping points.

Near the edge of a scan, one MODIS pixel can cover about 9.67× the ground it covers straight below (Giglio et al., 2016) and overlap 1 to 115 VIIRS pixels (Zheng et al., 2026).

04 · The question

And some of them aren’t fires at all

Hot bare ground, factories and sun glint all set off false alarms. Someone responding to a fire has to work out which points matter.

Ember gives that job to a machine-learning model.

How it works

From orbit to answer in five steps.

  1. 1

    Fetch

    Request MODIS and VIIRS detections for any area and up to 10 days from the FIRMS API.

  2. 2

    Parse

    Turn each CSV row into a typed detection with location, time, brightness and fire power.

  3. 3

    Harmonize

    Put both sensors on one timeline and one map, with their different confidence scales kept intact.

  4. 4

    Classify

    A Random Forest labels each detection as a real fire or a false alarm.

  5. 5

    Show & share

    Draw it on the map, inspect it, and export it for analysis.

The model

A Random Forest lets many decision trees vote

Each tree asks yes/no questions about a detection: its brightness, fire power, confidence, day or night, sensor, and how many other detections are nearby. Then the trees vote (Breiman, 2001).

Because it weighs several clues at once, it can flag a lone hot pixel over a factory roof without throwing away a small real fire.

Why the data is messy

Pixels stretch toward the edge of every scan

A sensor sweeps side to side as the satellite moves forward. Straight below, pixels are small. Toward the edge, each MODIS pixel covers far more ground and neighboring rows start to overlap, so one fire is detected again and again. Drag the slider and watch.

How we test it

Scored against fires that really happened

The model trains on one fire season and is scored on a different one it has never seen, using mapped fire perimeters as the answer key.

Experiment in progress: results coming soon

  1. MODIS + VIIRS detections, California, Jun–Nov 2020 and 2021
  2. Answer key: CAL FIRE fire perimeters
  3. Train on 2020, test on 2021 (unseen data)
  4. Score precision (of everything flagged as fire, how much really was) and recall (of all real fires, how many were caught)
  5. Compare with keeping every detection, unfiltered

The demo

Meet Ember.

A native macOS app built with SwiftUI and MapKit. It downloads live and archived detections from the NASA FIRMS API and shows every single one, exactly as reported. No hidden clustering, no silent filtering.

Data system

NASA FIRMS

The Fire Information for Resource Management System delivers active fire detections worldwide, typically within hours of a satellite pass.

  • Global, free API
  • Near-real-time + archive

Sensor

MODIS

Aboard Terra and Aqua. Over two decades of fire records, with a long, consistent history for training models.

  • ~1 km pixels
  • Confidence 0–100%

Sensor

VIIRS

Aboard Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21. Sharper pixels that catch smaller fires. Ember defaults to NOAA-20.

  • 375 m pixels
  • Confidence low / nominal / high
