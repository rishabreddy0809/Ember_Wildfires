01 · The planet is burning

Every point on this globe is a real fire detection

That’s all 136,676 detections NASA’s FIRMS system recorded from MODIS and NOAA-20 VIIRS on Nov 7–8, 2026. Most are in Africa, where seasonal burning is common.

Satellites don’t see flames. They spot thermal anomalies: pixels much hotter than the ground around them. Bigger points released more fire radiative power.

02 · Two eyes in the sky

MODIS and VIIRS see the ground differently

MODIS rides on Terra and Aqua with pixels about 1 km wide. VIIRS rides on Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 with pixels about 375 m wide (Giglio et al., 2016; Schroeder et al., 2014).

Each one scans a wide strip, called a swath, as it orbits. (Orbits drawn here are illustrations.)

03 · The messy part

One fire becomes many detections

Zoom into California on the same day: 133 detections. Stack both sensors on one map and a single fire turns into a pile of overlapping points.

Near the edge of a scan, one MODIS pixel can cover about 9.67× the ground it covers straight below (Giglio et al., 2016) and overlap 1 to 115 VIIRS pixels (Zheng et al., 2026).

04 · The question

Which ones are real wildfires?

Hot bare ground, factories, and sun glint all trigger false alarms. Ember makes the raw signal visible, then puts two methods head to head to sort real fires from noise.

Each sensor misses fires the other catches, so combining them makes sense. But combining them also piles up duplicates and false alarms that responders have to wade through.

Our solution

Meet Ember: raw satellite fire data you can actually read.

Ember is a native macOS app built with SwiftUI and MapKit. It downloads live and archived detections from the NASA FIRMS API and shows every single one, exactly as reported. No hidden clustering, no silent filtering.

⚡

Near-real-time

Pulls fresh MODIS and VIIRS detections straight from the NASA FIRMS API, with a built-in rate limiter and an offline cache.

◎

Nothing hidden

Every detection is drawn exactly as reported. MODIS as circles, VIIRS as diamonds, so you can see the overlaps for yourself.

⌖

Inspect anything

Click a point to see every FIRMS column. Overlapping detections? Step through them one at a time with [ and ].

⇩

Science-ready export

One keystroke (⌘E) exports a combined, harmonized CSV that loads straight into Python and pandas.

How it works

From orbit to answer in five steps.

Deep dive · Why duplicates happen

Pixels stretch toward the edge of every scan

A sensor sweeps side to side as the satellite moves forward. Straight below, pixels are small. Toward the edge, each MODIS pixel covers far more ground and neighboring rows start to overlap, so one fire gets detected again and again. Drag the slider and watch.

Method A · Clustering

DBSCAN groups detections in space and time

Real fires produce many detections close together. DBSCAN links detections within 1 km and 24 hours: groups become fires, loners become false alarms (Ester et al., 1996). Press Run DBSCAN and watch the weakness: a factory that’s hot every 12 hours looks like a fire too.

Method B · Machine learning

A Random Forest lets hundreds of trees vote

Each decision tree asks yes/no questions about a detection: its brightness, fire power, confidence, day or night, sensor, and how many neighbors it has. The trees vote (Breiman, 2001). Because it looks at more than location, it can catch the false alarms clustering misses.

The science

Clustering vs. machine learning: which one finds the real fires?

Does a Random Forest model or a spatial-temporal clustering algorithm more accurately identify real wildfires from combined MODIS and VIIRS data, measured against known wildfire perimeters?

Hypothesis: the Random Forest will score higher on precision and recall, because it can use extra clues about each detection to recognize false alarms that clustering would group as fires.

ApproachUsesStrengthWeakness
Clustering (DBSCAN)Location and timeFinds fires of any shapeRepeatedly hot factories look like fires
Random ForestSix features per detectionCombines many cluesOnly as good as its labels
ControlNothingSimple baselineKeeps every false alarm

Experiment design

  1. MODIS + VIIRS detections, California, Jun–Nov 2020 and 2021
  2. Ground truth: CAL FIRE fire perimeters
  3. Train on 2020, test on 2021 (unseen data)
  4. Score precision & recall, 6 monthly trials
  5. Compare methods with a paired t-test
